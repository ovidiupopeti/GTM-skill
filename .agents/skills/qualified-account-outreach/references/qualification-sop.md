# Generic qualification and outreach SOP

Use this SOP for any B2B seller. Seller-specific facts belong in the run input, never in the reusable skill.

## Input contract

The input should name:

- **Seller:** company, product or service, website and relevant first-party URLs.
- **Offer:** problem solved, credible proof, desired next step, and claims that must not be made.
- **ICP hypothesis:** target industries, scale, geography, operating conditions, buying roles, triggers, and disqualifiers. If omitted, derive a provisional version from seller-owned pages and label it assumption-based.
- **Target:** company name, website, and useful first-party URLs.
- **Output path:** where the brief should be written.
- **Optional permissioned enrichment:** local authorization record and either Apify search filters or LinkedIn URLs. Never commit these.

## Five-dimension scorecard

Score each dimension `0 = contrary/absent`, `1 = plausible but incomplete`, or `2 = strong cited evidence`.

1. **Firmographic fit:** industry, scale, geography, business model, technology, workforce, or operating complexity matches the seller's ICP.
2. **Problem evidence:** public facts support the problem the seller solves; do not confuse generic industry risk with company-specific evidence.
3. **Trigger or timing:** recent initiative, regulation, expansion, hiring, leadership statement, incident disclosure, procurement signal, or strategic change makes the problem timely.
4. **Solution and proof fit:** the seller's supported capabilities and proof plausibly map to verified target conditions without stretching claims.
5. **Buying-path clarity:** at least one plausible role, a credible organizational owner, and a low-friction next step can be identified without inventing an org chart.

Use `priority` for 8–10, `nurture` for 5–7, and `insufficient fit evidence` for 0–4. A high score is relevance, not intent or win probability. Always show the strongest counterargument and unknowns.

## Required output

1. Seller and target one-liners.
2. Decision, total score, and confidence.
3. Reproducible scorecard.
4. Evidence table: claim, URL, retrieval date, confidence, reason.
5. Likely primary and supporting buying roles.
6. Observable company communications tone with source support.
7. Signal-to-capability map separating facts from hypotheses.
8. Two-touch draft sequence.
9. Counterevidence and strongest reason not to pursue.
10. Unknowns and next qualification questions.
11. Sources and limitations.

## Outreach quality bar

The opening observation must be specific enough that it cannot be sent unchanged to every target, but not so personal that it feels monitored. Ask a diagnostic question instead of asserting hidden pain. Use one supported seller capability as the bridge. Mirror the target company's level of formality, not an inferred individual personality. Make the second touch useful even if the first was ignored. Stop before sending and recommend tracking replies and objections before claiming effectiveness.
