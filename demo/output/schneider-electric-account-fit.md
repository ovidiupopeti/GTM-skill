## Two-touch outreach hypothesis

### Touch 1

**Subject:** Measuring human risk across Schneider's four HRM layers

Your public HRM program already spans 160,000 employees and global, regional, role-based, and individual layers—including connected and non-connected workers. That is well beyond compliance-first awareness. One question at this maturity is how to unify behavioral signals and interventions across such a complex estate without adding program administration. Is that a priority for your team in 2026? OutThink connects adaptive training, real-time nudges, and human-risk intelligence. Would a 20-minute working session to compare approaches be useful?

### Touch 2

One other angle: Schneider's public program already tailors awareness by region, role, and behavior and extends into OT-heavy operations. OutThink's strongest relevance may therefore be orchestration and evidence—connecting those layers to measurable human-risk intelligence—rather than replacing basic awareness. Would it be more useful to compare the measurement layer, or confirm there is no current gap?

## Counterevidence and strongest reason not to pursue

Schneider Electric publicly describes a mature, comprehensive HRM program with role-based and individual interventions. It may already have strong internal capability or an incumbent platform. Without evidence of an unresolved measurement, orchestration, integration, or administration gap, high functional fit may not translate into an active opportunity.

## Unknowns to qualify before outreach

- Is Schneider Electric already an OutThink customer or active opportunity?
- Which platform or internal systems run the HRM program today?
- Are behavioral signals unified across awareness, SOC, IAM, GRC, and OT contexts?
- Which outcome is hardest to evidence: behavior change, human-risk reduction, engagement, or administrative efficiency?
- Is there a procurement, renewal, regulatory, or AI-threat trigger in the next 12 months?
